When your league switches to Playoff Mode, our system automatically ranks every team based on regular-season results. Two things matter most:

    Division winners
    Winning percentage

When Teams are Tied

If two or more teams finish with the same winning percentage, a series of tiebreakers kicks in. The system works through them one at a time until the tie is broken.

Here's the important part: tiebreakers are applied one seed at a time. Once the system settles who gets a seed, it starts the whole process over for the next one — and keeps going until every spot in the bracket is filled.

In ESPN public leagues, the order is:

    Points For
    Head-to-head record
    Intradivisional record
    Points Against
    Coin flip

In League Manager (LM) leagues, head-to-head record is usually the first tiebreaker (this FAQ assumes that setup). However, League Managers can reorder the tiebreakers however they like, so it is always best to check your league settings to be sure.
Seeding Tiebreaker Sequence

    H2H Record (overall) among the tied teams. If these teams have not all played each other an equal number of times, the H2H tiebreaker is invalid, and the process proceeds to Points For.
    Points For. (NOTE: This is the default first tiebreaker in all public leagues.) Most points wins. If a tie remains, the process proceeds to IntraDivisional Record.
    IntraDivisional Record (best H2H record within the same division). If a tie remains, the process proceeds to Points Against.
    Points Against. Most points wins.* If a tie remains, the process proceeds to a virtual coin flip.
    Coin Flip.

* In Fantasy Football you have no control over how many points your opponent scores against you, so Points Against serves as a proxy for the NFL's "strength of schedule" tiebreaker. Assuming a comparable schedule, the team with more Points Against played a stronger schedule and therefore wins the tie.

NOTE: Once a team has been seeded, the process starts over, beginning with H2H Record. This process repeats until all playoff brackets are filled.
Tiebreaker Process When Two Teams Are Tied

    Compare the H2H record between the two teams. Best record wins.
    If the records are the same, compare Points For.
    If Points For is tied, compare IntraDivisional Record.
    If IntraDivisional Record is tied, compare Points Against. Most points wins.
    If the teams remain tied, a virtual coin flip determines the seed.

Tiebreaker Process When Multiple Teams Are Tied

When more than two teams are tied, the process repeats for each remaining seed.

    Compare the H2H record of all tied teams. The team with the best H2H record takes the top seed.
    Among the remaining teams, compare H2H records again. If the remaining teams did not play each other an equal number of times, compare Points For; the team with the highest Points For takes the next seed.
    Repeat step 2 until all tied teams are seeded.

Example: How Seeding Tiebreakers Work

The main things to remember are:

    Teams must have played the same number of head-to-head games against each other for the H2H tiebreaker to apply.
    The tiebreaker process starts over from the beginning after each tie is broken.

All five teams in this example have a season record of 7-5.

    Team A: 2nd in Division 1, 825 Points For. Beat Team B; lost to Teams C, D, and E.
    Team B: 3rd in Division 1, 918 Points For. Beat Team C; lost to Teams A and E; did not play Team D.
    Team C: 1st in Division 2, 725 Points For. Beat Teams A and D; lost to Teams B and E.
    Team D: 2nd in Division 2, 720 Points For. Beat Teams A and E; lost to Team C; did not play Team B.
    Team E: 1st in Division 1, 920 Points For. Beat Teams A, B, and C; lost to Team D.

First, seed the division winners

Division 1 (Teams A, B, and E): all three finished 7-5, so head-to-head record decides the division. Team E beat both Team A and Team B (2-0), Team A went 1-1, and Team B went 0-2. Team E wins Division 1.

Division 2 (Teams C and D): both finished 7-5, and Team C beat Team D in their head-to-head matchup. Team C wins Division 2.

Team E beat Team C in their head-to-head matchup, so Team E gets the #1 seed, and Team C gets the #2 seed.

NOTE: Some LM leagues have no divisions. In that case, this step is skipped and, barring a tie, the top-ranked team is assigned the #1 seed.
Determine the #3 seed

The process starts over with the remaining teams. Teams A, B, and D all have 7-5 records, so begin by comparing their head-to-head records.

    Team A beat Team B and lost to Team D, so Team A is 1-1 head-to-head.
    Team B lost to Team A and did not play Team D, so Team B is 0-1 head-to-head.
    Team D beat Team A and did not play Team B, so Team D is 1-0 head-to-head.

IMPORTANT: Because these three teams did not play the same number of head-to-head games, the H2H tiebreaker is invalid. Move to the next tiebreaker, Points For.

Applying Points For:

    Team A has 825 Points For
    Team B has 918 Points For
    Team D has 720 Points For

Team B gets the #3 seed with the most Points For of the three remaining teams.
Determine the #4 seed

The process starts over again. Teams A and D have identical 7-5 records, so compare their head-to-head matchup. Team D beat Team A, so Team D gets the 4th and final playoff seed.
League Manager (LM) Leagues

The League Manager has the ultimate say on which teams advance to the playoffs. The default tiebreaker process is used, unless your League Manager has modified the tiebreaker system on the "Edit Playoffs" page in LM Tools.

If your league uses its own playoff tiebreakers, we highly recommend defining them before the season and posting them prominently on the League Office page. Click here to learn how to change Playoff Seeding in LM leagues.

NOTE: "Most Points from Bench" is available only in grandfathered LM Leagues that used this tiebreaker in past seasons.
